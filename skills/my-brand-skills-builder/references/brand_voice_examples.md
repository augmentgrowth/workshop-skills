# Brand Voice Examples

## Overview

Real-world examples of brand voice from well-known companies. Use these as reference and inspiration when developing brand voice guides, but always create authentic, original content that reflects the specific brand's unique personality.

---

## 1. Mailchimp - Friendly, Helpful, Approachable

### Voice Characteristics
- **Spectrum Position:**
  - Casual (8/10)
  - Playful (7/10)
  - Collaborative (9/10)
  - Simple (8/10)
  - Innovative (7/10)

### Personality Traits
- Fun but not childish
- Clever but not silly
- Helpful but not overbearing
- Expert but not bossy
- Weird but not inappropriate

### Language Guidelines

**Words We Use:**
- "You've got this"
- "Let's do this"
- "Here's the thing"
- "Pretty cool, right?"
- Active, direct language

**Words We Avoid:**
- Corporate jargon
- Overly technical terms without explanation
- Patronizing language
- Excessive exclamation points

### Example Copy

**Error Message:**
❌ Bad: "Error 404: The requested resource could not be found."
✅ Good: "Hmm. We looked everywhere, but we can't find what you're looking for. Let's try something else."

**CTA Button:**
❌ Bad: "Submit Form"
✅ Good: "Send My Campaign"

**Success Message:**
❌ Bad: "Operation completed successfully."
✅ Good: "Nice! Your campaign is on its way."

## 2. Stripe - Clear, Direct, Technical (but accessible)

### Voice Characteristics
- **Spectrum Position:**
  - Formal (4/10) - Professional but not stiff
  - Serious (7/10) - Business-focused
  - Authoritative (7/10) - Expert positioning
  - Technical (7/10) - Sophisticated but clear
  - Innovative (8/10) - Cutting-edge

### Personality Traits
- Direct and precise
- Sophisticated and intelligent
- Confident and authoritative
- Developer-friendly
- No fluff

### Language Guidelines

**Words We Use:**
- "Developer-first"
- "Build" (not "create")
- "Revenue" (concrete, measurable)
- Technical terms (explained clearly)
- Specific numbers and data

**Words We Avoid:**
- Marketing hyperbole
- Vague claims
- Cutesy language
- Unnecessary adjectives
- "Easy" or "simple" (show, don't tell)

### Example Copy

**Value Proposition:**
❌ Bad: "The amazing payment solution that makes it super easy to accept payments!"
✅ Good: "Infrastructure to accept payments, send payouts, and manage your business online."

**Feature Description:**
❌ Bad: "Our awesome dashboard lets you see everything!"
✅ Good: "View transactions, analyze revenue, and reconcile payments from a unified interface."

**Documentation:**
❌ Bad: "Let's get started with your first payment! It's going to be fun!"
✅ Good: "To accept your first payment, create a PaymentIntent with the amount and currency."

## 3. Innocent Drinks - Playful, Witty, Quirky

### Voice Characteristics
- **Spectrum Position:**
  - Casual (10/10) - Extremely conversational
  - Playful (9/10) - Humor and personality
  - Collaborative (8/10) - Friendly and inclusive
  - Simple (9/10) - No jargon
  - Innovative (6/10) - Fresh but approachable

### Personality Traits
- Cheeky and fun
- Humble and honest
- Optimistic and positive
- Slightly silly
- Human and conversational

### Language Guidelines

**Words We Use:**
- "Lovely"
- "Little"
- Conversational asides
- British colloquialisms
- Self-deprecating humor

**Words We Avoid:**
- Corporate speak
- Aggressive marketing language
- Anything too serious
- Pretentious words

### Example Copy

**Product Packaging:**
❌ Bad: "100% fruit juice with no added sugar. Premium quality ingredients."
✅ Good: "Nothing but nothing but fruit. Fruit and more fruit. We promise there are no trampolines in here."

**About Page:**
❌ Bad: "Founded in 1999, our company is committed to producing premium beverages."
✅ Good: "Way back in 1998, we sold our first smoothies at a little music festival in London. We put up a sign asking people if they thought we should give up our jobs to make smoothies. They said yes. We did."

**Customer Service:**
❌ Bad: "Thank you for your inquiry. A representative will assist you shortly."
✅ Good: "Hello you. Thanks for getting in touch. We'll email you back faster than you can say 'blueberries'."

## 4. Nike - Inspirational, Empowering, Bold

### Voice Characteristics
- **Spectrum Position:**
  - Casual (6/10) - Confident and direct
  - Serious (5/10) - Inspiring but not heavy
  - Authoritative (8/10) - Motivational leader
  - Simple (7/10) - Clear and powerful
  - Innovative (9/10) - Pushing boundaries

### Personality Traits
- Motivational and inspiring
- Bold and confident
- Inclusive and empowering
- Action-oriented
- Authentic athletic

### Language Guidelines

**Words We Use:**
- "You"
- "Your"
- Active verbs
- Short, powerful sentences
- Imperative form

**Words We Avoid:**
- Passive language
- Qualifiers and hedging
- Corporate speak
- Over-explanation
- Self-congratulation

### Example Copy

**Campaign Headline:**
❌ Bad: "Our innovative new running shoes are designed to help you perform at your best."
✅ Good: "Run faster. Jump higher. Win."

**Product Description:**
❌ Bad: "These shoes feature our latest technology for enhanced performance."
✅ Good: "Built for speed. Made for you."

**Social Media:**
❌ Bad: "We hope everyone is having a great training session today!"
✅ Good: "What did you do today to get better?"

## 5. Slack - Professional, Friendly, Human

### Voice Characteristics
- **Spectrum Position:**
  - Casual (7/10) - Conversational but professional
  - Playful (6/10) - Optimistic with personality
  - Collaborative (9/10) - Partnership-focused
  - Simple (8/10) - Clear communication
  - Innovative (7/10) - Modern approach

### Personality Traits
- Helpful and supportive
- Optimistic and positive
- Clear and straightforward
- Empathetic
- Slightly witty

### Language Guidelines

**Words We Use:**
- "Work"
- "Team"
- "Together"
- "Your" (customer-focused)
- Action words

**Words We Avoid:**
- Jargon and acronyms
- Corporate buzzwords
- Exclamation points (sparingly)
- Passive voice
- Complexity

### Example Copy

**Value Proposition:**
❌ Bad: "Revolutionize your enterprise communications with our cutting-edge collaborative platform."
✅ Good: "Slack is where work happens."

**Feature Intro:**
❌ Bad: "Introducing our advanced threaded messaging functionality."
✅ Good: "Threads help you organize conversations and make decisions without the noise."

**Empty State:**
❌ Bad: "No items to display."
✅ Good: "You're all caught up! Nice work."

## 7. Wendy's (Social Media) - Sassy, Bold, Humorous

### Voice Characteristics
- **Spectrum Position:**
  - Casual (10/10) - Extremely conversational
  - Playful (10/10) - Witty and humorous
  - Authoritative (7/10) - Confident, sometimes snarky
  - Simple (9/10) - Direct and punchy
  - Innovative (8/10) - Unexpected for fast food

### Personality Traits
- Witty and sarcastic
- Bold and fearless
- Self-aware
- Competitive
- Authentic and human

### Language Guidelines

**Words We Use:**
- Internet vernacular
- Roasts and comebacks
- Pop culture references
- Direct callouts
- Honest opinions

**Words We Avoid:**
- Corporate PR speak
- Overly polite language
- Generic responses
- Fake enthusiasm

### Example Copy

**Twitter Response to Competitor:**
❌ Bad: "We believe our fresh, never frozen beef sets us apart from the competition."
✅ Good: "Your beef is frozen and you should feel bad."

**Customer Interaction:**
❌ Bad: "Thank you for choosing Wendy's! We hope you enjoyed your meal."
✅ Good: "You're welcome. We know you'll be back."

**Product Announcement:**
❌ Bad: "Introducing our new spicy chicken sandwich, now available nationwide."
✅ Good: "Your tweets worked. Spicy nuggs are back. Don't mess this up again."

### Language Guidelines

- Corporate jargon

❌ Bad: "Automated workflow capabilities enable efficient customer support operations."
✅ Good: "Answer common questions automatically, so your team can focus on the hard stuff."

## How to Use These Examples

### 1. Analyze What Works
- What makes each voice distinctive?
- How do they maintain consistency?
- What language patterns emerge?

### 2. Identify Patterns for Your Brand
- Which examples align with your brand personality?
- What can you adapt (not copy) for your voice?
- How do they handle similar scenarios?

### 3. Create Your Own Examples
- Use these as inspiration, not templates
- Develop authentic examples for your brand
- Test against your unique voice spectrum

### 4. Avoid Direct Copying
- These are reference points, not templates
- Your brand needs its own authentic voice
- Adapt principles, not exact phrasing

---

## Red Flags to Avoid

**Voice Inconsistency:**
- Being playful on social but formal on website
- Different tone in different product areas
- Personality that changes with different writers

**Forced Personality:**
- Trying to be funny when it's not natural
- Copying another brand's voice too closely
- Personality that doesn't match brand values

**Over-the-Top:**
- Too casual for professional contexts
- Too many exclamation points
- Trying too hard to be different
