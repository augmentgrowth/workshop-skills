# Messaging Frameworks

## Overview

Messaging frameworks organize a brand's core communications into a structured hierarchy. Choose the framework that best fits the brand's complexity, audience, and market positioning.

---

## Framework 1: Classic Pyramid (Simple & Versatile)

**Best For:** Most brands, especially those with clear value propositions

### Structure

```
                    [Tagline/Positioning]
                          ↓
                [Core Value Proposition]
                          ↓
            [3-5 Key Message Pillars]
                          ↓
              [Proof Points for Each]
```

### Components

**Tagline/Positioning Statement**
- One memorable phrase that captures brand essence
- 5-8 words max
- Example: "Think Different" (Apple), "Just Do It" (Nike)

**Core Value Proposition**
- 1-2 sentences explaining what the brand delivers and to whom
- Should answer: What do you do? Who for? What makes you different?
- Example: "Slack is the collaboration hub that brings the right people, information, and tools together to get work done—from anywhere."

**Key Message Pillars (3-5)**
- Main themes that support the value proposition
- Each pillar represents a distinct benefit or differentiator
- Example Pillars for a project management tool:
  1. Simplicity that scales
  2. Team collaboration without chaos
  3. Visibility that drives accountability

**Proof Points**
- Specific evidence supporting each pillar
- Can be features, stats, customer stories, methodology
- Example for "Simplicity that scales":
  - Set up in under 5 minutes
  - Supports teams from 2 to 2,000
  - 98% user satisfaction in ease-of-use

## Framework 2: Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Framework

**Best For:** Product-focused brands, SaaS, tools with clear use cases

### Structure

Focus on the "job" the customer is hiring your product to do:

```
When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]
```

### Components

**Functional Job**
- The practical task the customer needs to accomplish
- Example: "When I need to share files with my team..."

**Emotional Job**
- How the customer wants to feel
- Example: "...I want to feel confident they're secure..."

**Social Job**
- How the customer wants to be perceived
- Example: "...so my team sees me as organized and professional."

### Message Architecture

**Core Message:** We help [audience] [accomplish job] when [situation]

**Supporting Messages:**
1. Without the [pain/friction] of [old solution]
2. With [key differentiator] that ensures [emotional benefit]
3. So you can [ultimate outcome/transformation]

**Example (Project Management Tool):**
- Core: We help teams ship projects on time when deadlines are tight
- Without the complexity of enterprise tools
- With intuitive workflows that make collaboration effortless
- So you can focus on great work, not project management

## Framework 3: Before-After-Bridge (Transformation)

**Best For:** Brands solving clear problems, transformation stories

### Structure

```
BEFORE: Current painful state
AFTER: Desired future state
BRIDGE: How we get you there
```

### Components

**Before State**
- Paint the pain picture
- Show the struggle, frustration, or limitation
- Make it relatable and specific
- Example: "Your team is drowning in email threads, losing important updates, and working in silos."

**After State**
- Show the transformation
- Describe the ideal outcome
- Make it aspirational but believable
- Example: "Your team collaborates in real-time, with full visibility, and ships projects 40% faster."

**Bridge**
- Explain how your solution creates the transformation
- Connect the dots between before and after
- Highlight key differentiators
- Example: "Our unified workspace brings conversations, files, and tasks into one place—with powerful automation that eliminates busywork."

## Framework 4: Three Cs (Problem-Agitate-Solve)

**Best For:** Challenger brands, disruptors, addressing broken status quo

### Structure

```
COMPLICATION: The problem everyone faces
CONSEQUENCE: Why it matters (urgency)
CURE: Your solution
```

### Components

**Complication**
- Identify the widely-recognized problem
- Make it specific and relatable
- Example: "Traditional CRMs are built for sales managers, not salespeople."

**Consequence**
- Amplify why this problem matters
- Show the cost of inaction
- Create urgency
- Example: "So sales teams waste 4 hours a week on data entry instead of selling, and managers still can't trust the pipeline data."

**Cure**
- Present your solution as the remedy
- Show how it's fundamentally different
- Emphasize the transformation
- Example: "Built for the way salespeople actually work, with automation that captures every interaction and gives managers real-time, accurate visibility."

## Framework 5: Feature-Advantage-Benefit (FAB)

**Best For:** Technical products, feature-rich solutions, B2B sales

### Structure

For each major capability:

```
FEATURE: What it is
ADVANTAGE: How it works / what it does
BENEFIT: Why it matters / what you gain
```

### Components

**Feature**
- The specific capability or functionality
- Objective, factual description
- Example: "Real-time collaboration"

**Advantage**
- What makes this feature unique or better
- The differentiator vs. competition
- Example: "Multiple people can edit simultaneously with instant sync, no version conflicts"

**Benefit**
- The outcome or value the customer experiences
- Connect to business or personal goals
- Example: "Ship documents 3x faster without the back-and-forth of email attachments"

**Framework Application:**
Organize 5-7 key features this way, grouping by theme:
- Efficiency features
- Collaboration features
- Security features
- etc.

## Framework 6: Hero's Journey (Narrative)

**Best For:** Mission-driven brands, category creators, storytelling-focused brands

### Structure

```
HERO: Your customer
CHALLENGE: What they face
GUIDE: Your brand (mentor role)
PLAN: The path forward
SUCCESS: The transformation
```

### Components

**Hero (The Customer)**
- Position the customer as the protagonist
- Example: "Growth marketers in Series A startups"

**Challenge**
- The dragon to slay, the mountain to climb
- Example: "Trying to scale acquisition without scaling budget"

**Guide (Your Brand)**
- You're the mentor, not the hero
- Show empathy and expertise
- Example: "We've helped 500+ startups navigate this exact challenge"

**Plan**
- The clear path you provide
- 3-5 steps to success
- Example:
  1. Audit current channel efficiency
  2. Identify high-ROI opportunities
  3. Test and scale systematically

**Success**
- The victorious outcome
- Transformation achieved
- Example: "3x MRR growth in 12 months without increasing CAC"

## Choosing the Right Framework

### Decision Tree

**Is your value proposition simple and clear?**
- Yes → Classic Pyramid

**Are you solving a very specific job?**
- Yes → Jobs-to-be-Done

**Is there a clear before/after transformation?**
- Yes → Before-After-Bridge

**Are you disrupting a broken status quo?**
- Yes → Three Cs (Problem-Agitate-Solve)

**Do you have multiple technical features?**
- Yes → Feature-Advantage-Benefit

**Are you building a movement or category?**
- Yes → Hero's Journey

### Can You Combine Frameworks?

Yes! Many brands use:
- Classic Pyramid for overall messaging
- Jobs-to-be-Done for specific use cases
- FAB for product pages
- Before-After-Bridge for case studies

## Examples by Framework

### Classic Pyramid Example: Notion

**Positioning:** "All-in-one workspace"

**Value Prop:** "Notion is the connected workspace where better, faster work happens."

**Pillars:**
1. Write, plan, and get organized
2. Built for teams of any size
3. Customizable for any workflow

**Proof Points:**
- Docs, wikis, projects in one place
- Real-time collaboration
- 50+ templates, infinite customization

---

### Jobs-to-be-Done Example: Calendly

**Job:** When I need to schedule meetings, I want to eliminate email back-and-forth, so I can focus on the actual conversation, not the logistics.

**Messages:**
- No more "Does Tuesday work for you?" email chains
- Your availability, automatically synced and shared
- Professional scheduling that respects everyone's time

---

### Before-After-Bridge Example: Superhuman

**Before:** Email takes hours of your day. Your inbox is overwhelming. You can't find what you need.

**After:** You achieve inbox zero daily. Email becomes a superpower, not a burden.

**Bridge:** Superhuman is the fastest email experience ever made, with keyboard shortcuts, AI triage, and blazing speed that makes email feel effortless.

## Creating Your Messaging Framework

1. **Choose Your Framework** based on brand fit
2. **Fill in Each Component** with brand-specific content
3. **Validate with Examples** - write real sample content using the framework
4. **Test Across Channels** - does it work for website, sales, support?
5. **Document as Reference** - make it easy for team to use
