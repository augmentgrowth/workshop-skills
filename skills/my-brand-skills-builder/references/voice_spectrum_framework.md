# Voice Spectrum Framework

## Overview

The voice spectrum helps position a brand's communication style across key dimensions. Each spectrum represents a sliding scale where brands can position themselves to create a distinctive and consistent voice.

## The Five Core Spectrums

### 1. Formal ←→ Casual

**Formal (Left)**
- Uses complete sentences and proper grammar
- Avoids contractions, slang, and colloquialisms
- Professional and polished language
- Maintains distance and authority

**Casual (Right)**
- Conversational and relaxed tone
- Uses contractions, everyday language
- Feels like talking to a friend
- More approachable and accessible

**Example Comparison:**
- **Formal:** "We would be delighted to assist you with your inquiry."
- **Casual:** "We'd love to help you out!"

**Positioning Guidance:**
- Financial services, legal, healthcare → More formal
- Consumer tech, lifestyle brands, entertainment → More casual
- B2B enterprise → Moderate to formal
- B2C apps and services → Moderate to casual

---

### 2. Serious ←→ Playful

**Serious (Left)**
- Focuses on facts, data, and results
- Professional and businesslike
- Minimal humor or personality
- Emphasizes credibility and expertise

**Playful (Right)**
- Incorporates humor and wit
- Shows brand personality and character
- Uses creative language and wordplay
- Makes topics fun and engaging

**Example Comparison:**
- **Serious:** "Our platform reduces customer churn by 23% through predictive analytics."
- **Playful:** "We help you keep the customers you worked so hard to win. No breakups on our watch!"

**Positioning Guidance:**
- Healthcare, finance, security → More serious
- Creative agencies, consumer brands → More playful
- Enterprise software → Moderate, with playful moments
- Education, wellness → Balanced approach

---

### 3. Authoritative ←→ Collaborative

**Authoritative (Left)**
- Positions brand as the expert
- Tells audience what to do
- Confident and directive language
- "We know best" positioning

**Collaborative (Right)**
- Partners with the audience
- Invites input and conversation
- "We're in this together" positioning
- Acknowledges shared challenges

**Example Comparison:**
- **Authoritative:** "Follow these five steps to improve your SEO rankings."
- **Collaborative:** "Let's work together to boost your SEO. Here's what we've learned..."

**Positioning Guidance:**
- Consulting, advisory services → More authoritative
- Community platforms, collaborative tools → More collaborative
- SaaS tools → Balanced, leaning collaborative
- Medical/legal advice → More authoritative with empathy

---

### 4. Technical ←→ Simple

**Technical (Left)**
- Uses industry terminology
- Assumes audience knowledge
- Detailed and precise language
- Shows deep expertise

**Simple (Right)**
- Explains in plain language
- Avoids jargon and acronyms
- Accessible to beginners
- Focuses on benefits over features

**Example Comparison:**
- **Technical:** "Our ML-powered NLP engine leverages transformer architectures for semantic analysis."
- **Simple:** "Our AI understands what your customers mean, not just what they say."

**Positioning Guidance:**
- Developer tools, B2B tech → More technical
- Consumer products → More simple
- Consider audience: technical buyers vs. end users
- Use technical for credibility, simple for clarity

---

### 5. Traditional ←→ Innovative

**Traditional (Left)**
- Proven, established, reliable
- References heritage and experience
- Conservative and safe language
- "Time-tested" positioning

**Innovative (Right)**
- Forward-thinking and cutting-edge
- Emphasizes what's new and different
- Bold and adventurous language
- "Future-focused" positioning

**Example Comparison:**
- **Traditional:** "For over 50 years, we've helped businesses succeed with proven strategies."
- **Innovative:** "We're reimagining what's possible with breakthrough technology."

**Positioning Guidance:**
- Legacy brands, banks, law firms → More traditional
- Startups, tech companies, disruptors → More innovative
- Balance: Can be innovative in approach but traditional in values
- Consider industry disruption level

---

## How to Use the Spectrums

### Step 1: Plot Your Position
For each spectrum, determine where the brand falls on a 1-10 scale:
- 1-3: Strongly toward left
- 4-6: Balanced/moderate
- 7-10: Strongly toward right

### Step 2: Justify Each Position
For each placement, explain:
- **Why:** What about the brand, audience, or industry drives this choice?
- **Context:** When might this shift (e.g., more serious in crisis communications)?
- **Examples:** Show what this sounds like in practice

### Step 3: Create Visual Representation
Suggest a simple text-based visualization:

```
Formal ────●─────────── Casual
        (3/10 - Professional but approachable)

Serious ─────────────●─ Playful
              (8/10 - Optimistic with personality)
```

### Step 4: Test Against Real Content
Review existing content and ask:
- Does it align with these spectrum positions?
- Where are the inconsistencies?
- What needs to change?

## Red Flags

**Avoid These Spectrum Conflicts:**
- Very casual + very authoritative = Feels arrogant or condescending
- Very technical + very playful = Can seem frivolous or unprofessional
- Very traditional + very innovative = Confusing mixed signals
- Very formal + very simple = Can feel patronizing

**Look for Natural Alignments:**
- Casual + collaborative + simple = Approachable and helpful
- Formal + authoritative + technical = Expert and credible
- Playful + innovative + simple = Fresh and accessible
- Serious + technical + authoritative = Professional and expert
